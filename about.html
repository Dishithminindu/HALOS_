Scientific Methodology & Architecture Protocol

1. Scientific Rationale & Assessment Protocol

Addressing high dietary sodium intake through dual-source observational modeling

Excessive dietary sodium is a primary modifiable risk factor for hypertension and cardiovascular diseases. Traditional 24-hour urinary sodium excretion remains the clinical gold standard but suffers from high participant burden and day-to-day intra-individual variation.

HALOS (Habitual & Acute Lithium/Sodium Observational Suite) integrates high-resolution 24-hour multi-meal dietary recall with a 30-day semi-quantitative Food-Frequency Questionnaire (FFQ). This dual approach captures both acute daily variance and longitudinal habitual exposure to high-sodium foods (such as salted dried fish, Maldive fish sambols, pickles, instant noodles, and processed condiments).

2. Isolated Multi-Tier ML Architecture

Zero client-side simulation • Server-authoritative inference pipeline
[Static Frontend UI]
    ↓ (HTTPS REST / JSON)
[Cloudflare Worker Gateway & Auth Router]
    ↓ (D1 Feature Aggregation)
[Python FastAPI Microservice (ml-service)]
    ↓ (Joblib Model Deserialization)
[Trained Random Forest Regressor & Classifier Ensemble]
    ↓ (Prediction Interval & Importance Vector)
[Cloudflare D1 Database Persistence] → [Audit Logging] → [Client Render]

To maintain strict scientific integrity, all machine learning inference, ensemble tree voting, and feature engineering are executed exclusively within the Python backend using scikit-learn, NumPy, pandas, and FastAPI. The browser client performs only input collection, local validation, and verified result rendering.

3. Stoichiometry & Metric Definitions

Standardized chemical conversions and epidemiological terminology
  • Sodium to Salt Equivalent Conversion: Based on the atomic weight ratio of sodium (23 g/mol) and chlorine (35.5 g/mol) in NaCl (58.5 g/mol):
    Salt (g/day) = (Sodium (mg/day) × 2.5) / 1000
  • Reference Percentage: Calculated as (Predicted Salt / 5.0g) × 100. This is an intake comparison metric relative to public health recommendations, not an indicator of machine-learning accuracy or confidence.
  • Risk Classifications:
    • ▼ LOWER INTAKE: < 5.0 g salt/day (< 2,000 mg sodium/day)
    • ● MODERATE INTAKE: 5.0 – 7.0 g salt/day (2,000 – 2,800 mg sodium/day)
    • ▲ HIGHER INTAKE: > 7.0 g salt/day (> 2,800 mg sodium/day)

4. Research Prototype Disclaimer & Limitations

HALOS v2.0 is an academic and epidemiological research prototype. Estimates generated by the Random Forest models are designed to assist researchers in population-level dietary assessment and risk stratification.

Outputs do not constitute a clinical or medical diagnosis. For clinical management of hypertension or electrolyte balance, physicians must rely on comprehensive clinical evaluations, formal urinary sodium assays, and validated medical diagnostic protocols.
